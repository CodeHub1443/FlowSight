# Task 21: /live remount leak (navigation leak)

Main worktree, baseline `021fa94` (task-20.9). Not committed.

## Root cause
A three.js `WebGLRenderer` adds a `dispose` listener to every material, geometry and texture it uploads
(`onMaterialDispose`, `onGeometryDispose`, `onTextureDispose`). Only that resource's own `dispose()` removes the listener.
`renderer.dispose()` does not, and neither does R3F's `forceContextLoss()` on unmount. Each listener closes over the renderer, its GL
context and its canvas. Per-mount resources were already disposed in effect cleanups, so geometry and texture counts stayed flat. Every
resource that outlives the scene, however, kept every dead renderer alive. Through its canvas (`parentNode` chain), each dead renderer
also kept the whole detached /live DOM tree: about 900 nodes, their listeners and one WebGL canvas per visit.

Retainer path, leak 1 (the shortest; it held all three dead trees in a 3-cycle heap snapshot):
```
GC root -> scene chunk module scope .ACTOR_SOLID_MATERIAL (MeshStandardMaterial, src/scene/actors/pool.ts)
  ._listeners.dispose[k] -> closure onMaterialDispose (one per renderer that ever drew it)
  .context -> WebGLRenderer constructor scope .canvas -> <canvas data-engine="three.js r186"> (detached)
  .parentNode ... -> the old /live subtree (bay pills, truck plates, tray rows, KPI chips, icons)
```
Leak 2 is the same mechanism on every other shared resource. Each one carried 4 listeners = 4 renderers (3 dead + the live one):
- **Shared materials:** `ACTOR_BOX_MATERIAL`, `BOX_MATERIAL`, `CYL_MATERIAL`, `BOX_DEPTH_MATERIAL`.
- **Shared geometry:** `UNIT_PLANE`, the box/cylinder/sphere caches (`geometry.ts`), `bay/brackets.ts`, the `shapes.ts` cache, EventPin `BODY`/`CORE`.
- **Label textures:** the `labels/textures.ts` CanvasTexture cache.
- **GLTF parts:** held in R3F's `useLoader` cache.
- **Third party:**
  - three r186's module-level DFG lookup texture (`getDFGLUT()`, bound into every MeshStandardMaterial).
  - n8ao's module-level `FullScreenTriangleGeometry`. `N8AOPostPass` inherits postprocessing's `Pass.dispose()`, which never disposes
    the pass's quads.

Ruled out:
- CameraRig keyboard/pointer/focus listeners, SceneRoot `visibilitychange`, and the ResizeObserver/matchMedia in `useRenderPixelRatio`:
  all removed in their cleanups.
- uiStore and bus subscriptions: they return their unsubscribes.
- ECharts: disposed (20.9).

Measuring note: an unreleased `Runtime.queryObjects` result holds the canvases it returns (the "DevTools console" handle). Counts must
release the object group, as `e2e/t21-nav-leak.spec.ts` does.

## Fix
- `src/scene/gpuRelease.ts`:
  - `sharedAcrossMounts()` registers module-level resources.
  - `collectSceneResources(gl, scene)` collects:
    - the registry;
    - geometry and materials (`customDepthMaterial` included) reachable from the scene;
    - the textures of those materials;
    - the textures the renderer bound into their uniforms (`gl.properties.get(m).uniforms`), which reaches three's DFG LUT.
  - `disposeAll()` disposes the collection.
  - `disposeFullScreenQuads(pass)` handles n8ao's quads.
- `src/scene/useReleaseOnUnmount.ts`: SceneRoot sets the ref in `onCreated`. On unmount the hook collects while the scene graph is still
  whole, then disposes one task later, and only if the effect did not re-run (StrictMode's cleanup-and-setup disposes nothing).
- Registered resources:
  - `actors/pool.ts`: actor materials.
  - `geometry.ts`: `UNIT_PLANE`, the unit caches, the box/cylinder materials and the depth material.
  - `shapes.ts`, `bay/brackets.ts`, `labels/textures.ts`, `labels/EventPin.tsx`: their caches and shared geometry.
  - `assets/loader.ts`: GLTF part geometries and materials.
- `ScenePost.tsx`: N8AO gets a stable module-level callback ref whose cleanup disposes the pass's quads.
- `dispose()` frees GPU state only. CPU data stays, and the next renderer uploads it unchanged. Verified pixel-identical on fresh loads and
  after remounts (below).

## Gates (architect decision, replaces the brief's "+2 MB at 10 cycles")
- **Strict counts, every checked cycle:** nodes within +3 %, listeners within +5 %, ALL canvases in the heap (detached included) equal to
  the start, renderer geometries and textures equal. These are the leak gate.
- **Settle before the start sample (both modes):** after scene ready, force GC and poll the heap every 2 s until two consecutive samples
  differ by under 0.3 MB (cap 30 s; a hit is logged and annotated).
- **Default, 10 cycles:** heap sanity ceiling of +5 MB over the settled start. It was +4 MB. The settled runs measured +3.2 to +3.9 MB
  (+6.6 MB before the fix), which left under 1 MB of headroom, so it flaked in practice.
- **`T21_LONG=1`, 50 cycles (the authoritative heap gate):** slope under 0.1 MB per cycle over cycles 20 to 50, and at most +3 MB over the
  settled start at cycle 50. It samples cycles 10, 20, 25, ... 50; cycle 10 keeps the +5 MB sanity ceiling.
- **Why the 10-cycle heap is only a sanity check:** over the first visits the heap is dominated by warm-up (JIT code, Chrome's capped
  timing buffers, the live renderer's state; it peaks near cycle 15 and then levels off). At 10 cycles the leak (+6.63 MB before the fix)
  and the fix (+2.6 to +3.9 MB, depending on when the start is sampled) are too close to separate with a fixed ceiling. The counts separate
  them at any cycle (before the fix nodes went 1,016 -> 9,761 and canvases 13 -> 22 in 10 cycles; after, all flat), and so does the slope
  once the warm-up is over (0.429 against 0.007 MB/cycle).

## Nav latency and the canvas wait (architect decision, second round)
- **Spec change:** the wait for the scene canvas to leave after the push to /trucks is 20 s (was 5 s). It is not part of the gate.
  - Each cycle logs the in-page time from the push to the canvas leaving the document: a MutationObserver set up just before the
    push, `performance.now()` deltas.
  - The run prints min/median/max after the loop.
  - A cycle over 5 s gets a Playwright annotation `slow-nav` with the cycle number and its duration.
  - No pause after settle.
- **Router:** `src/app/App.tsx` uses react-router 7.18.4's `<BrowserRouter>` with no `useTransitions` prop. Its history listener calls
  `React.startTransition(() => setStateImpl(newState))` unless `useTransitions === false`, so every navigation, the spec's popstate
  included, is a React transition. No app code calls `startTransition`/`useTransition`. `/trucks` is not lazy.

## Third round: launch flags tried (refuted, not adopted), 120 s backstop
Architect hypothesis: the slow leaves, the page not answering for over 20 s and the intermittent map-controls failure come from Windows
occlusion or background throttling of the test browser, not from the fix. **Refuted. The flags were tried and are not adopted, and
`playwright.config.ts` is unchanged (no diff against HEAD).**

- **The Playwright config:**
  - It has no `headless` key and no `--headed`, so Chromium runs headless. Playwright 1.64.1 uses `chromium-headless-shell` for that.
  - Launch args: `--use-gl=angle`, `--use-angle=swiftshader`, `--enable-unsafe-swiftshader`, `--ignore-gpu-blocklist`.
- **Tried for the round-3 runs, then removed:** `--disable-backgrounding-occluded-windows`, `--disable-renderer-backgrounding`,
  `--disable-background-timer-throttling`, `--disable-features=CalculateNativeWinOcclusion`.
- **Why they cannot matter here:**
  - The headless shell has no native window, so Windows window occlusion and backgrounding have nothing to act on.
  - Playwright already passes the first three by default (`chromiumSwitches` in playwright-core).
  - Playwright appends user args after its own `--disable-features=<list>` (Translate, PaintHolding, ThirdPartyStoragePartitioning and
    others). Chromium keeps the last value of a repeated switch, so the fourth probably replaced that list instead of adding to it.
    Not verified.
- **Spec (kept):**
  - The leave wait is a 120 s backstop. The test timeout follows it: `CYCLES * (120 s + 90 s) + 30 s + 120 s`, which is 37 min by default
    and 2.95 h in long mode.
  - Every heap sample (start, cycle 10, cycles 20 to 50 in steps of 5 in long mode, final) and every cycle's leave time is printed when it
    is taken. Each line also goes to `test-results/t21-nav-leak.log`.
  - Unchanged: the `slow-nav` annotation (> 5 s), the counts, +5 MB at cycle 10, +3 MB at cycle 50 and the slope gate.

### Nav-latency finding
- **The slow leave remains unexplained.** In SwiftShader the median leave is about 10 s (9.5 to 13.1 s) at about 10 % CPU before the run: 9.5 s and
  11.3 s with the flags, 13.1 s without them. The launch flags do not explain it (above).
- **The runs do not separate the remaining candidates:**
  - Round 2 failed on the 20 s wait; the 120 s backstop is why no run fails on the wait any more.
  - The CPU before the runs was lower (about 10 % against 20 to 35 % in round 2).
  - The earlier passing run (round 1, 19.8 % CPU) left within 5 s on every cycle.
- **Map controls** passed 5 of 5 with the flags present. Before, it was 1 of 2 on the current tree and passed once on the baseline. It was
  not rerun without the flags.
- **Router-transition hypothesis, unverified:** every navigation is a React transition (`<BrowserRouter>` without `useTransitions={false}`
  calls `React.startTransition`), so the /live tree, its 4 Hz store flushes and its SwiftShader frames stay mounted until that transition
  commits. Nothing here confirms or rules this out. The backlog has the item to measure it on the client PC's real GPU.
